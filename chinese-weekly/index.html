Grammar

1. Result complements (Verb + 完 / 好 / 见 / 到)

Chinese separates the attempt from the outcome. 完 answers "is the action over?"; 好 answers "is the result done and good?"; 见 means perceived (看见 = looked and actually saw); 到 means reached/attained. 写完报告 means you stopped writing; 写好报告 means it's finished and ready to send. Without a complement the sentence often sounds unfinished to a native ear. Negate with 没 or 还没 — never 不 — and drop the 了. Structure: Verb + 完/好/见/到 (+ 了).

我看完电影了。🔊
Wǒ kànwán diànyǐng le.
I've finished watching the movie.
报告我写好了。🔊
Bàogào wǒ xiěhǎo le.
The report is written and ready.
我看见这个店还有汤。🔊
Wǒ kànjiàn zhège diàn hái yǒu tāng.
I see this restaurant still has soup.

2. 过 — the experience marker

了 reports a completed event; 过 reports an accumulated experience. 我吃了羊肉炒饭 means I ate it (on that occasion); 我吃过羊肉炒饭 means I have eaten it before, ever. The negative always uses 没, and 过 stays in place — 没吃过, never 不吃过. Useful for talking about travel, food and anything on a life résumé. Structure: Subject + Verb + 过 + Object (negative: 没 + Verb + 过).

我没吃过羊肉炒饭。🔊
Wǒ méi chī guo yángròu chǎofàn.
I've never eaten lamb fried rice.
你用过饿了么吗？🔊
Nǐ yòng guo Èleme ma?
Have you ever used Ele.me?
我学过一点儿中文。🔊
Wǒ xué guo yìdiǎnr Zhōngwén.
I've studied a little Chinese before.

3. Adjective + 的 standing in for a noun

甜的 literally is "sweet's," i.e. the sweet one. The full form 甜的东西 is always available, but once both speakers know what is being discussed the 东西 or the noun drops out. This is why the whole taste list — 甜的、酸的、辣的、咸的、苦的、烫的 — works as a standalone answer to "what don't you like?" The same trick works with any adjective. Structure: Adjective + 的 (= adjective + 东西/noun, with the noun dropped).

我不喜欢吃甜的。🔊
Wǒ bù xǐhuan chī tián de.
I don't like eating sweet things.
我要一杯冰的。🔊
Wǒ yào yì bēi bīng de.
I'd like an iced one.
我要辣的，不要咸的。🔊
Wǒ yào là de, bú yào xián de.
I want the spicy one, not the salty one.

4. 太 + adjective (+ 了)

很辣 is just "very spicy," a neutral description; 太辣了 is "too spicy," a complaint. The 了 is near-obligatory in speech and carries the sense of "has gone past the line." 太好了 is the one common positive use, where the excess is a good thing. Structure: 太 + Adjective + 了.

这个菜太咸了。🔊
Zhège cài tài xián le.
This dish is too salty.
太好了，我们点外卖吧。🔊
Tài hǎo le, wǒmen diǎn wàimài ba.
Great — let's order delivery.
这个汤太烫了。🔊
Zhège tāng tài tàng le.
This soup is scalding hot.

5. 让 + person + verb (let, have, or ask someone to do something)

让 takes a person as its object and then a whole second verb phrase describing what that person does. The English can be let, have, make or ask depending on tone, but the Chinese structure never changes. The negative 不让 is the natural way to say someone won't allow something — 不 goes before 让, not before the main verb. Structure: A + 让 + B + Verb; negative: A + 不让 + B + Verb.

老板让我帮他买咖啡。🔊
Lǎobǎn ràng wǒ bāng tā mǎi kāfēi.
My boss asked me to buy him coffee.
妈妈不让我玩手机游戏。🔊
Māma bú ràng wǒ wán shǒujī yóuxì.
Mom doesn't let me play phone games.
让孩子回答我的问题。🔊
Ràng háizi huídá wǒ de wèntí.
Let the child answer my question.

6. 帮 + person + verb — doing something on someone's behalf

帮 takes the person directly, with no 的 and no preposition, and the action follows. Unlike English "help," 帮 here often means doing it instead of them, not assisting alongside them — 我帮你点 is "I'll place the order for you." It combines readily with 让: 让 + person + 帮 + person + verb stacks two relationships into one sentence. The separable noun version is 帮忙 (帮我一个忙), which takes no second verb. Structure: Subject + 帮 + person + Verb phrase.

你可以帮我做一件事情吗？🔊
Nǐ kěyǐ bāng wǒ zuò yí jiàn shìqing ma?
Can you help me do something?
我帮你点。🔊
Wǒ bāng nǐ diǎn.
I'll order it for you.
帮我点一个鸡蛋汤吧。🔊
Bāng wǒ diǎn yí ge jīdàn tāng ba.
Please order an egg soup for me.

7. 发给 and 给 + person + 发 (sending something to someone)

Both orders are natural. 给 introduces the recipient; when it sits after the verb it attaches to it as 发给. If the thing sent is already clear from context it drops out, leaving just 发给 + person. 发 covers messages, emails, files and reports alike. Structure: 发 + thing + 给 + person; or 给 + person + 发 + thing.

请你发给王经理。🔊
Qǐng nǐ fā gěi Wáng jīnglǐ.
Please send it to Manager Wang.
给老板发报告。🔊
Gěi lǎobǎn fā bàogào.
Send the report to the boss.
我给经理发了一条信息。🔊
Wǒ gěi jīnglǐ fā le yì tiáo xìnxī.
I sent the manager a message.

8. 已经 + result complement + 了

我写完了 states the completion; 已经写完了 adds "already," often answering a question or heading off a request. 已经 goes before the verb and 了 closes the sentence — both are needed for the pattern to sound natural. Structure: Subject + 已经 + Verb + result complement + 了.

我已经写完了。🔊
Wǒ yǐjīng xiěwán le.
I've already finished writing it.
我已经做完今天的工作了。🔊
Wǒ yǐjīng zuòwán jīntiān de gōngzuò le.
I've already finished today's work.
我已经吃完饭了。🔊
Wǒ yǐjīng chīwán fàn le.
I've already finished eating.

9. 还没 + result complement (not yet)

This is the mirror image of 已经……了. Use 没 or 没有, never 不, to negate a completed result, and drop the sentence-final 了. 还 adds "yet," implying the completion is still expected. Structure: Subject + 还没(有) + Verb + result complement.

我还没做完。🔊
Wǒ hái méi zuòwán.
I haven't finished it yet.
我还没写完报告。🔊
Wǒ hái méi xiěwán bàogào.
I haven't finished writing the report yet.
我还没吃好。🔊
Wǒ hái méi chīhǎo.
I haven't finished eating yet.

10. 了: completed action, change of state, completed result

Don't collapse 了 into one English word. 我吃了药 reports a completed action. 我生病了 reports a new state — I'm sick now. 我看完了 attaches 了 to a result complement, presenting the finished result as relevant right now. Structure: Verb + 了 + object; Sentence + 了; Verb + result complement + 了.

我休息了一天。🔊
Wǒ xiūxi le yì tiān.
I rested for a day.
我生病了。🔊
Wǒ shēngbìng le.
I've gotten sick.
我请假了。🔊
Wǒ qǐngjià le.
I've taken time off.

11. Measure words 件 and 份

Learn these as fixed pairings for now rather than as general rules. 件 counts things in the sense of matters, tasks or affairs — 一件事情, 两件事情. 份 counts documents you would hand over as a copy — 一份报告. Note the sandhi: 一 becomes yí before the fourth-tone 件 and 份. Structure: Number + 件 + 事情; Number + 份 + 报告.

我有两件事情要告诉你。🔊
Wǒ yǒu liǎng jiàn shìqing yào gàosu nǐ.
I have two things to tell you.
他写了一份报告。🔊
Tā xiě le yí fèn bàogào.
He wrote a report.
这份报告很重要。🔊
Zhè fèn bàogào hěn zhòngyào.
This report is important.

12. 比 comparison with 一点

The adjective takes no 很 in a 比 sentence. 一点 after the adjective softens the gap to "a little"; leave it off and the comparison is flat. For "about the same" rather than a difference, switch to 跟……差不多. Structure: A + 比 + B + Adjective + 一点.

今天比昨天忙一点。🔊
Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān máng yìdiǎn.
Today is a little busier than yesterday.
这个工作比那个工作无聊一点。🔊
Zhège gōngzuò bǐ nàge gōngzuò wúliáo yìdiǎn.
This job is a little more boring than that one.
这个店比那个店贵一点。🔊
Zhège diàn bǐ nàge diàn guì yìdiǎn.
This shop is a little more expensive than that one.

13. 跟……差不多 (about the same as)

Where 比 marks a difference, 跟……差不多 marks near-sameness. It is the natural answer when someone asks whether next week will be busier: not more, not less, 差不多. 差不多 can also stand alone as a reply meaning "about the same." Structure: A + 跟 + B + 差不多.

下个星期跟这个星期差不多。🔊
Xià ge xīngqī gēn zhège xīngqī chàbuduō.
Next week will be about the same as this week.
我的中文跟他差不多。🔊
Wǒ de Zhōngwén gēn tā chàbuduō.
My Chinese is about the same as his.
这个汤跟那个汤差不多。🔊
Zhège tāng gēn nàge tāng chàbuduō.
This soup is about the same as that one.

14. 很 and 有点 + adjective

Chinese adjectives work as predicates on their own, so no 是 appears. 很 is the neutral default — 很忙 often just means "busy," not "very busy." 有点 means "a little" and carries a mildly negative flavour, so 有点忙 and 有点无聊 sound like complaints. Structure: Subject + 很 + Adjective; Subject + 有点 + Adjective.

我今天很累。🔊
Wǒ jīntiān hěn lèi.
I'm tired today.
这个工作有点无聊。🔊
Zhège gōngzuò yǒudiǎn wúliáo.
This job is a little boring.
这个汤有点酸。🔊
Zhège tāng yǒudiǎn suān.
This soup is a little sour.

15. 身体 + 好/不好 (asking about health)

身体 literally means body, but 你的身体好吗？ is the ordinary question about health, not an anatomical one. 健康 also means health or healthy, though it is less common in this question. Answer with 我的身体很好 or 我的身体不好. Structure: 你的身体好吗？; Subject + 的身体 + 很好 / 不好.

你的身体好吗？🔊
Nǐ de shēntǐ hǎo ma?
How is your health?
我的身体不好，昨天生病了。🔊
Wǒ de shēntǐ bù hǎo, zuótiān shēngbìng le.
My health isn't good; I got sick yesterday.
我的身体很好。🔊
Wǒ de shēntǐ hěn hǎo.
My health is good.

16. 怎么: how versus how come

Context decides which reading applies. Before a plain verb, 怎么 asks by what means — 你怎么回家？ Before a negated verb it asks "how come" and carries surprise or mild reproach — 你怎么没来上班？ The negative is the tell. Structure: Subject + 怎么 + Verb (manner); Subject + 怎么 + 没/不 + Verb (surprise).

你每天怎么回家？🔊
Nǐ měitiān zěnme huí jiā?
How do you get home every day?
你怎么没来上班？🔊
Nǐ zěnme méi lái shàngbān?
Why didn't you come to work?
你怎么用支付宝付钱？🔊
Nǐ zěnme yòng Zhīfùbǎo fù qián?
How do you pay with Alipay?

17. 多 and 少 + verb (giving advice)

多 and 少 sit before the verb, not after, and no 一点 is needed. Adding 要 turns the phrase into advice: 要多休息. The paired form 少……，多…… is the standard shape for health and study advice, and it is one of the most productive spoken patterns in this lesson. Structure: (要 +) 多 + Verb; (要 +) 少 + Verb.

你要多休息，多喝水，多吃水果。🔊
Nǐ yào duō xiūxi, duō hē shuǐ, duō chī shuǐguǒ.
You should rest more, drink more water, and eat more fruit.
你要少看手机，多看书。🔊
Nǐ yào shǎo kàn shǒujī, duō kàn shū.
You should use your phone less and read more.
你要多复习生词。🔊
Nǐ yào duō fùxí shēngcí.
You should review vocabulary more.

18. Time + place + verb + 了 + duration

The place phrase with 在 goes before the verb; the duration goes after it. English puts both after the verb ("rested at home for a day"), which is why 我休息了一天在家 is such a common mistake. Keep the order: time, place, verb, duration. Structure: (Time) + Subject + 在 + Place + Verb + 了 + Duration.

昨天我在家休息了一天。🔊
Zuótiān wǒ zài jiā xiūxi le yì tiān.
Yesterday I rested at home for a day.
我在公司工作了八个小时。🔊
Wǒ zài gōngsī gōngzuò le bā ge xiǎoshí.
I worked at the company for eight hours.
今天我在家午休了半个小时。🔊
Jīntiān wǒ zài jiā wǔxiū le bàn ge xiǎoshí.
I took a half-hour nap at home today.

19. Verb + 一下 — softening an action

一下 means "once, briefly" and takes the edge off an imperative — 找 sounds like an order, 找一下 sounds like "just have a quick look." With physical actions it is literal: 点一下 is one click, 点两下 is a double-click. Native speakers use it constantly in instructions. Structure: Verb + 一下 (or Verb + 两下 for twice).

我找一下。🔊
Wǒ zhǎo yíxià.
Let me have a look.
点两下鼠标。🔊
Diǎn liǎng xià shǔbiāo.
Double-click the mouse.
我们试一下这个软件。🔊
Wǒmen shì yíxià zhège ruǎnjiàn.
Let's give this app a try.

20. Separable verbs (离合词): 请假, 放假, 帮忙, 开会, 生病

These words look like single verbs but are really verb + object underneath: 请假 is "request leave," 生病 is "produce illness." Anything measuring the action goes between the halves, never after the whole word. So it is 请三天假, not 请假三天; 生了两天病, not 生病了两天. Getting this wrong is one of the most audible intermediate mistakes. Structure: Verb-half + [number/measure/duration/了] + Noun-half.

你想请几天假？🔊
Nǐ xiǎng qǐng jǐ tiān jià?
How many days off do you want to take?
我生了两天病。🔊
Wǒ shēng le liǎng tiān bìng.
I was sick for two days.
我明天要开一个会。🔊
Wǒ míngtiān yào kāi yí ge huì.
I have a meeting tomorrow.

21. 先……然后……(再/就) — sequencing steps

先 marks the first step and sits after the subject, before the verb. 然后 introduces the next step and can be reinforced by 再 (and then, next) or 就 (and then right away). This is the backbone of any how-to explanation, which is why the app walkthrough uses it repeatedly. Structure: 先 + Verb Phrase 1，然后 + (再/就) + Verb Phrase 2.

你先打开这个软件，然后点一下这里。🔊
Nǐ xiān dǎkāi zhège ruǎnjiàn, ránhòu diǎn yíxià zhèlǐ.
First open this app, then tap here.
点一下确定，现在我们就可以点外卖了。🔊
Diǎn yíxià quèdìng, xiànzài wǒmen jiù kěyǐ diǎn wàimài le.
Tap Confirm, and now we can order delivery.
你先选一个菜，然后点一下确定。🔊
Nǐ xiān xuǎn yí ge cài, ránhòu diǎn yíxià quèdìng.
First choose a dish, then tap Confirm.

22. 放假 vs 假期 — the verb and the noun for time off

放假 is what happens — 放 release + 假 leave — so it behaves like a verb and takes time words in front: 我下个星期放假. 假期 is the period, a noun, so it takes 的 and adjectives: 我的假期很短. If the English sentence is "I'm off next week," use 放假; if it is "my vacation is short," use 假期. Structure: Subject + 放假 (verb) ／ Possessive + 假期 (noun).

我下个星期放假。🔊
Wǒ xià ge xīngqī fàngjià.
I'm off next week.
假期你要去哪里？🔊
Jiàqī nǐ yào qù nǎlǐ?
Where are you going over the holiday?
今年的假期很短。🔊
Jīnnián de jiàqī hěn duǎn.
This year's holiday is short.

23. 确定 vs 决定 — confirm vs. decide

确定 means to make certain, to verify, to be sure — it is the word on the OK button in an app, and it also works as an adjective (我不确定 = I'm not sure). 决定 means to make a decision and is normally followed by what you decided to do. Test: if the English is "confirm" or "be certain," use 确定; if it is "decide to," use 决定. Structure: 确定 + (Object/Clause) ／ 决定 + Verb Phrase.

写好以后点一下确定。🔊
Xiě hǎo yǐhòu diǎn yíxià quèdìng.
After you finish writing, tap Confirm.
我决定明天放假。🔊
Wǒ juédìng míngtiān fàngjià.
I've decided to take tomorrow off.
我不确定他决定了没有。🔊
Wǒ bù quèdìng tā juédìng le méiyǒu.
I'm not sure whether he's decided yet.

24. 热 vs 烫 — warm vs. scalding

热 covers the whole pleasant range — hot weather, hot soup you can drink, a warm room. 烫 is specifically hot enough to hurt: scalding water, food straight out of the pot. Asking for 热的水 gets you hot water to drink; 烫的水 is water that will burn your mouth. Structure: 热 + Noun ／ 烫 + Noun; 太烫了.

我吃饭不喜欢太烫的。🔊
Wǒ chīfàn bù xǐhuan tài tàng de.
When I eat, I don't like things that are too hot.
汤还是热的，可以喝了。🔊
Tāng háishi rè de, kěyǐ hē le.
The soup is still warm — you can drink it now.
我喜欢喝热的汤，不喜欢喝烫的汤。🔊
Wǒ xǐhuan hē rè de tāng, bù xǐhuan hē tàng de tāng.
I like drinking warm soup, not scalding soup.
