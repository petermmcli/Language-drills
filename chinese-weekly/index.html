Grammar

1. 把 Construction (把字句) — fronting a known object

把 (bǎ) pulls a definite object forward so the rest of the sentence can describe what happens to it. Structure: Subject + 把 + Object + Verb + result / destination / complement. The object must be specific and known to both speakers — you cannot say 我把一本书买了. The verb can almost never stand bare: it normally needs a result, direction, location, or other complement attached (放在…, 关上, 写下来, 洗干净, 给他).

请把手机放在包里。🔊
Qǐng bǎ shǒujī fàng zài bāo lǐ.
Please put the phone in the bag.
请把杯子洗干净。🔊
Qǐng bǎ bēizi xǐ gānjìng.
Please wash the cup clean.
请把门关上。🔊
Qǐng bǎ mén guān shàng.
Please close the door.

2. 因为…所以… (Because A, so B)

Links a cause and its consequence in a single sentence. Structure: （因为）+ reason + （所以）+ result. Unlike English, Chinese happily uses BOTH halves of the pair — 因为 and 所以 together is standard, not redundant. Either half can be dropped when the relationship is obvious. The reason clause comes first; the result clause follows.

因为下雨，所以我们没去滑雪。🔊
Yīnwèi xiàyǔ, suǒyǐ wǒmen méi qù huáxuě.
Because it rained, we didn't go skiing.
因为这条路很危险，所以我开车送孩子去学校。🔊
Yīnwèi zhè tiáo lù hěn wēixiǎn, suǒyǐ wǒ kāichē sòng háizi qù xuéxiào.
Because this road is dangerous, I drive the kids to school.
因为他病了，所以没来上课。🔊
Yīnwèi tā bìng le, suǒyǐ méi lái shàngkè.
Because he was sick, he didn't come to class.

3. A 比 B + adjective + degree (comparison with 比)

States that A exceeds B in some quality, and optionally says by how much. Structure: A + 比 + B + adjective + 一点儿 / 一些 / 多了 / 很多. The degree word goes AFTER the adjective, never before it — you cannot say A 比 B 很热. 一点儿 and 一些 mean "a little"; 多了 and 很多 mean "much more." Leaving the slot empty gives a plain comparison.

四川菜比上海菜辣一点儿。🔊
Sìchuān cài bǐ Shànghǎi cài là yìdiǎnr.
Sichuan food is a bit spicier than Shanghai food.
夏天比冬天热多了。🔊
Xiàtiān bǐ dōngtiān rè duōle.
Summer is much hotter than winter.
他比我忙很多。🔊
Tā bǐ wǒ máng hěn duō.
He is a lot busier than me.

4. A 没有 B + adjective (negative comparison)

Says that A does not reach B's level of some quality — the standard negative counterpart to 比. Structure: A + 没有 + B + adjective. 没有 here is not "don't have"; it's "not as … as." No degree word follows the adjective in this pattern, since the point is falling short rather than exceeding. 那么 or 这么 can be inserted before the adjective for emphasis.

上海菜没有四川菜辣。🔊
Shànghǎi cài méiyǒu Sìchuān cài là.
Shanghai food is not as spicy as Sichuan food.
写汉字没有说汉语容易。🔊
Xiě Hànzì méiyǒu shuō Hànyǔ róngyì.
Writing characters is not as easy as speaking Chinese.
今天没有昨天冷。🔊
Jīntiān méiyǒu zuótiān lěng.
Today is not as cold as yesterday.

5. 让 vs 请 (A 让/请 B + verb — having vs asking someone do something)

Both put a second person between the subject and the verb, but they differ in politeness and in who holds the authority. Structure: A + 让 + B + verb (make / have / let); A + 请 + B + verb (politely ask / invite / hire). 让 (ràng) is neutral and directive — A causes, tells, arranges for, or permits B to act. 请 (qǐng) is polite and stresses that B is being asked rather than directed; in commercial contexts it stretches to "invite" or even "hire."

她让她的朋友帮我找。🔊
Tā ràng tā de péngyou bāng wǒ zhǎo.
She had her friend help me look for it.
她请她的朋友帮我找。🔊
Tā qǐng tā de péngyou bāng wǒ zhǎo.
She asked her friend to help me find it.
老师让我们写作业。🔊
Lǎoshī ràng wǒmen xiě zuòyè.
The teacher had us do homework.
