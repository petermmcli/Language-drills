Grammar

1. 宾语前置 (bīnyǔ qiánzhì) — object fronting for emphasis

Moving the object to the front of the sentence makes it the topic — "as for X, I …" — and draws attention to it. Chinese has no case endings, so the object can be lifted to the front without any extra word; the rest of the sentence keeps its order, subject then verb. The teacher's phrasing was that you put the object forward to catch attention while "the rest part still keeps the order." It works best when the thing fronted is already known to both speakers — the character on the screen, the question just asked. Note that this is not a passive: the doer still sits right before the verb, and you keep only one subject pronoun (早饭 (zǎofàn) 我 (wǒ) 没吃 (méi chī), not 我 (wǒ) 早饭 (zǎofàn) 我 (wǒ) 没吃 (méi chī)). Structure: Object + Subject + Verb (instead of the neutral Subject + Verb + Object).

这个汉字我不认识。🔊
Zhège Hànzì wǒ bú rènshi.
I don't recognize this character.
这些阅读题我没有做完。🔊
Zhèxiē yuèdú tí wǒ méiyǒu zuò wán.
These reading questions, I haven't finished.
这个问题我已经回答了。🔊
Zhège wèntí wǒ yǐjīng huídá le.
This question, I've already answered.

2. 动词短语 + 的 + 名词 (dòngcí duǎnyǔ + de + míngcí) — a verb phrase describing a noun

Turns a whole verb phrase into a modifier, doing the work of an English "who / which / that" clause. English puts the clause after the noun ("the patients I see"); Chinese puts it before and ties it on with 的 (de), so the noun always lands last. The clause can be as long as you like — verb, adverb, object, result complement — as in 还没吃完的菜 (hái méi chī wán de cài). The finished phrase can then serve as subject or topic. One rule the teacher stressed: a thing is not a place, so describing someone's position needs 在 (zài) plus a direction word — 坐在椅子上的人 (zuò zài yǐzi shang de rén), never 坐椅子的人 (zuò yǐzi de rén). Structure: Verb phrase + 的 (de) + Noun.

这个穿白衬衫的人是我的朋友。🔊
Zhège chuān bái chènshān de rén shì wǒ de péngyou.
The person wearing the white shirt is my friend.
诊所我看的病人，我做手术。🔊
Zhěnsuǒ wǒ kàn de bìngrén, wǒ zuò shǒushù.
The patients I see at the clinic, I operate on.
还没吃完的饭放在冰箱里。🔊
Hái méi chī wán de fàn fàng zài bīngxiāng lǐ.
The food that isn't finished yet is in the fridge.

3. 别 / 不要，别…了 (bié / búyào, bié…le) — don't, and stop doing it

Gives a negative command — either forbidding an action generally, or asking someone to stop something already in progress. 别 (bié) is the short spoken contraction of 不要 (búyào) ("do not want"), and the teacher treated the two as interchangeable, with 别 (bié) the more colloquial choice. A time or situation clause with 的时候 (de shíhou) comes first, then the command. The final 了 (le) marks a change of state: the action is happening right now and you want it to end, which is why 别吃 (bié chī) ("don't eat") and 别吃了 (bié chī le) ("stop eating") are not the same. Never put 的 (de) after 别 (bié). Structure: 别 (bié) + Verb, or 不要 (búyào) + Verb; add 了 (le) at the end for "stop doing it".

骑车的时候别听音乐。🔊
Qí chē de shíhou bié tīng yīnyuè.
Don't listen to music while riding.
现在已经很晚了，别看手机了。🔊
Xiànzài yǐjīng hěn wǎn le, bié kàn shǒujī le.
It's already late, stop looking at your phone.
别担心，病人没事。🔊
Bié dānxīn, bìngrén méishì.
Don't worry, the patient is fine.

4. 怎么 + 还 (zěnme + hái) — why + still

Asks "why on earth haven't you …?" — a why-question carrying surprise or impatience. 怎么 (zěnme) here means "why", not "how", and differs from the neutral 为什么 (wèishénme) by adding a note of surprise. 还 (hái) sits directly before what it modifies and the negative 不 (bù) follows it, giving "still not" — the sense of a wait that has gone on too long. For something still in progress, add 在 (zài): 还在加班 (hái zài jiābān). The natural answers reuse the same pieces: 我还有很多工作 (wǒ hái yǒu hěn duō gōngzuò), 我等一下就回家 (wǒ děng yíxià jiù huí jiā). Keep the subject before 怎么 (zěnme). Structure: Subject + 怎么 (zěnme) + 还 (hái) + 不 (bù) / 没 (méi) + Verb.

你怎么还不回家？🔊
Nǐ zěnme hái bù huí jiā?
Why haven't you gone home yet?
现在已经很晚了，你怎么还在加班？🔊
Xiànzài yǐjīng hěn wǎn le, nǐ zěnme hái zài jiābān?
It's already very late, why are you still working overtime?
病人怎么还没来？🔊
Bìngrén zěnme hái méi lái?
Why hasn't the patient come yet?

5. 太 + 形容词 + 了 (tài + xíngróngcí + le) — too, excessive

Says that something goes past what is good or acceptable — "too expensive", "too cold", "too late". The 了 (le) here is simply part of the fixed frame, not the change-of-state particle, so it stays even when nothing has changed; the teacher was explicit that "太 (tài) adjective 了 (le) means too." The pattern carries a mild complaint or warning, which is why it pairs naturally with 别 (bié): 别回家太晚了 (bié huí jiā tài wǎn le). Contrast 很贵 (hěn guì) ("expensive", neutral) with 太贵了 (tài guì le) ("too expensive", a complaint), and 非常 (fēicháng) ("very"), which emphasises without the sense of excess. Structure: 太 (tài) + Adjective + 了 (le).

苹果手机太贵了。🔊
Píngguǒ shǒujī tài guì le.
Apple phones are too expensive.
题太多了，我没做完。🔊
Tí tài duō le, wǒ méi zuò wán.
There were too many questions, I didn't finish.
现在太晚了，别加班了。🔊
Xiànzài tài wǎn le, bié jiābān le.
It's too late now, stop working overtime.

6. 结果补语 完 / 到 / 懂，没 without 了 (jiéguǒ bǔyǔ wán / dào / dǒng, méi) — result complements, and 没 takes no 了

The second syllable states how the action turned out — finished it, understood it, or managed to get it. 完 (wán) means the whole thing got finished; 到 (dào) means the result was actually obtained. The teacher's illustration: if breakfast is served at seven and you arrive at eight thirty, you 没吃到早饭 (méi chī dào zǎofàn) — you never got any — whereas 没吃完 (méi chī wán) means you started and stopped partway. The 懂 (dǒng) complement must match the sense involved: 听懂 (tīng dǒng) for listening, 看懂 (kàn dǒng) for reading. 都 (dōu) goes before the verb phrase (问题我都看懂了 (wèntí wǒ dōu kàn dǒng le)) and before a time word (手术都七点开始 (shǒushù dōu qī diǎn kāishǐ)). The negative takes no 了 (le), since 没 (méi) already does that work. Structure: Verb + Result (做完 (zuò wán), 吃完 (chī wán), 看懂 (kàn dǒng), 听懂 (tīng dǒng), 吃到 (chī dào)); negative is 没 (méi) + Verb + Result, with no 了 (le).

很多问题我没听懂。🔊
Hěn duō wèntí wǒ méi tīng dǒng.
There were many questions I didn't understand.
我没吃到早饭。🔊
Wǒ méi chī dào zǎofàn.
I didn't get to eat breakfast.
问题我都看懂了。🔊
Wèntí wǒ dōu kàn dǒng le.
I understood all the questions.

7. 动词 + 错 (dòngcí + cuò) — doing something wrong, and who did it

Says that an action was carried out but the result was wrong, and the verb you choose names who made the mistake. 错 (cuò) here is a result complement, the same family as 完 (wán), 懂 (dǒng) and 到 (dào) — the verb says what was done, 错 (cuò) says how it turned out. The same problem produces three different sentences depending on who acted: 卖家发错了 (màijiā fā cuò le) (the seller sent it), 买家选错了 (mǎijiā xuǎn cuò le) (the buyer picked it), 快递员送错了 (kuàidìyuán sòng cuò le) (the courier delivered it). 了 (le) marks that it has happened; the negative uses 没 (méi) with no 了 (le) (没发错 (méi fā cuò)). When the thing itself is the subject, 错了 (cuò le) goes straight after it: 颜色错了 (yánsè cuò le), the colour is wrong. Structure: Subject + Verb + 错 (cuò) + 了 (le) (发错了 (fā cuò le) / 选错了 (xuǎn cuò le) / 送错了 (sòng cuò le) / 买错了 (mǎi cuò le)); Thing + 错了 (cuò le).

不好意思，我们发错了。🔊
Bù hǎoyìsi, wǒmen fā cuò le.
Sorry, we sent the wrong one.
快递员送错了。🔊
Kuàidìyuán sòng cuò le.
The courier delivered it to the wrong person.
卖家发错了尺码。🔊
Màijiā fā cuò le chǐmǎ.
The seller sent the wrong size.

8. 种 versus measure words (zhǒng) — kind-of vs. counting individual items

种 (zhǒng) counts kinds of thing; a measure word counts individual items. 种 (zhǒng) sits in the measure-word slot but means "type", so it works with any noun at all — 这种花 (zhè zhǒng huā), 这种菜 (zhè zhǒng cài), 这种手机 (zhè zhǒng shǒujī), 这种水 (zhè zhǒng shuǐ). A real measure word (盆 (pén) a pot, 支 (zhī) a stem, 件 (jiàn) a garment) names the unit you are buying, which is why a price sentence can need both: 这种花15块一支 (zhè zhǒng huā shíwǔ kuài yì zhī). A measure word can never stand in front of a noun on its own — 盆花 (pén huā) is wrong; you need 一盆花 (yì pén huā) or 这盆花 (zhè pén huā). Structure: 这 (zhè) / 那 (nà) / 一 (yī) + 种 (zhǒng) + Noun; Number + measure word + Noun.

这种花，大的230，小的180。🔊
Zhè zhǒng huā, dà de èrbǎi sānshí, xiǎo de yìbǎi bāshí.
This kind of flower: the big one is 230, the small one 180.
我买了两件衣服。🔊
Wǒ mǎi le liǎng jiàn yīfu.
I bought two pieces of clothing.
我买了三支花。🔊
Wǒ mǎi le sān zhī huā.
I bought three stems of flowers.

9. 怎么走 versus 怎么去 (zěnme zǒu, zěnme qù) — asking for a route vs. asking how you travel

怎么走 (zěnme zǒu) asks for directions; 怎么去 (zěnme qù) asks how you travel there. 走 (zǒu) is moving along a path, so 怎么走 (zěnme zǒu) gets you left, right and straight on. 怎么去 (zěnme qù) gets you 走路去 (zǒulù qù), 开车去 (kāichē qù), 骑车去 (qí chē qù). Directions are then built as a chain of short subjectless steps joined by 然后 (ránhòu) and 以后 (yǐhòu), exactly like instructions in English. Structure: Place + 怎么走 (zěnme zǒu)？(which route); 怎么去 (zěnme qù) + Place？(by what means).

请问4号楼怎么走？🔊
Qǐngwèn sì hào lóu zěnme zǒu?
Excuse me, how do I get to Building 4?
您进门左转，然后一直走，4号楼在您左边。🔊
Nín jìn mén zuǒ zhuǎn, ránhòu yìzhí zǒu, sì hào lóu zài nín zuǒbian.
Go in the gate and turn left, then go straight; Building 4 is on your left.
你怎么去医院？🔊
Nǐ zěnme qù yīyuàn?
How do you get to the hospital?

10. 快递 is a noun (kuàidì) — 发 and 送 are its verbs

快递 (kuàidì) names the parcel or the delivery service, so a separate verb has to carry the action. The seller 发 (fā) it, the courier 送 (sòng) it, you 收到 (shōudào) it. For timing, the parcel becomes the subject: 快递什么时候到 (kuàidì shénme shíhou dào)？Between people, 送 (sòng) + thing often reads as giving a present (我送你一件衣服 (wǒ sòng nǐ yí jiàn yīfu)), so a seller replacing an item says 发给 (fā gěi) or 给 (gěi), not 送 (sòng). Structure: 发 (fā) / 送 (sòng) + 快递 (kuàidì); 快递 (kuàidì) + 什么时候 (shénme shíhou) + 到 (dào)？; 发给 (fā gěi) + person.

你的快递到了。🔊
Nǐ de kuàidì dào le.
Your parcel has arrived.
我的快递还没到。🔊
Wǒ de kuàidì hái méi dào.
My parcel hasn't arrived yet.
他们今天发快递。🔊
Tāmen jīntiān fā kuàidì.
They're sending the parcel today.

11. 好评 / 差评，and 亲 (hǎopíng / chàpíng, qīn) — reviews, and shopping-app chat

Gives you the vocabulary of online reviews and the register of seller-to-buyer chat. 评 (píng) is short for 评价 (píngjià) or 评论 (pínglùn), your opinion of how something was; 好 (hǎo) or 差 (chà) in front says which kind. The verbs are 写 (xiě) and 给 (gěi), as in "write / give a review". 亲 (qīn) is clipped from 亲爱的 (qīn'ài de) and belongs only to buyer-seller chat on apps like 淘宝 (Táobǎo) — never to your wife and never face to face in a shop. 亲爱的 (qīn'ài de) is the one for people you are close to, and 亲人 (qīnrén) means family members. Structure: 写 (xiě) / 给 (gěi) + 好评 (hǎopíng) / 差评 (chàpíng); 五星好评 (wǔ xīng hǎopíng); 亲 (qīn) + message.

收到了记得帮我们好评。🔊
Shōudào le jìde bāng wǒmen hǎopíng.
When you get it, remember to give us a good review.
亲，有问题再找我哦。🔊
Qīn, yǒu wèntí zài zhǎo wǒ o.
Dear, if there's a problem, come to me again.
很多地方都是五星好评。🔊
Hěn duō dìfang dōu shì wǔ xīng hǎopíng.
Lots of places are five-star reviewed.

12. 放到 + thing + 里 (fàngdào + lǐ) — turning a thing into a place

An object has to be made into a location with 里 (lǐ) or 上 (shàng) before it can be where something goes. A car, a chair or a bag is a thing, not a place, so 在你的车 (zài nǐ de chē) is incomplete — it needs 车里 (chē lǐ). The same rule gives 椅子上 (yǐzi shang), 床上 (chuáng shang), 包里 (bāo lǐ). With 放 (fàng) the full pattern is 放到 (fàngdào) + thing + 里 (lǐ), put it into. Structure: 放到 (fàngdào) + Noun + 里 (lǐ) / 上 (shàng); 在 (zài) + Noun + 里 (lǐ) / 上 (shàng).

衣服放到车里。🔊
Yīfu fàng dào chē li.
Put the clothes in the car.
我的手机在包里。🔊
Wǒ de shǒujī zài bāo li.
My phone is in my bag.
您放在这儿吧。🔊
Nín fàng zài zhèr ba.
Just put it here.

13. 比 with 很多 after the adjective (bǐ) — much more [adjective] than

Says that A is much more [adjective] than B. In a 比 (bǐ) sentence the degree word goes after the adjective, never before it — 漂亮很多 (piàoliang hěn duō) or 漂亮多了 (piàoliang duō le), not 很漂亮 (hěn piàoliang) or 更漂亮 (gèng piàoliang) in that slot. A small difference works the same way: 漂亮一点儿 (piàoliang yìdiǎnr). Structure: A + 比 (bǐ) + B + Adjective + 很多 (hěn duō) / 多了 (duō le).

李爱的家比我的家漂亮很多。🔊
Lǐ Ài de jiā bǐ wǒ de jiā piàoliang hěn duō.
Li Ai's home is much prettier than mine.
这种手机比那种贵多了。🔊
Zhè zhǒng shǒujī bǐ nà zhǒng guì duō le.
This kind of phone is much more expensive than that one.
宽面条比细面条好吃。🔊
Kuān miàntiáo bǐ xì miàntiáo hǎochī.
Wide noodles taste better than thin ones.

14. 让 — getting someone else to do something (ràng) — let / make / want someone to

Says that one person wants, lets or makes another person do something. 让 (ràng) introduces a second subject-verb pair inside the sentence; the person after 让 (ràng) is the one who actually does the action. It covers let, make and want-someone-to, and the main verb in front of it sets the tone. Structure: Subject + 想 (xiǎng) / 要 (yào) + 让 (ràng) + Person + Verb.

卖家想让李爱做什么？🔊
Màijiā xiǎng ràng Lǐ Ài zuò shénme?
What does the seller want Li Ai to do?
医生让我多喝水。🔊
Yīshēng ràng wǒ duō hē shuǐ.
The doctor told me to drink more water.
老板让我加班。🔊
Lǎobǎn ràng wǒ jiābān.
The boss made me work overtime.

15. 除非 — unless (chúfēi) — the one condition that would change the result

States the one condition under which the result would be different. 除非 (chúfēi) singles out the only exception, and the second half usually carries 不然 (bùrán) or 否则 (fǒuzé), otherwise. It is stronger than 如果不 (rúguǒ bù) because it insists that this is the single case that counts. Structure: 除非 (chúfēi) + condition，不然 (bùrán) / 否则 (fǒuzé) + result.

除非有问题，不然我不退货。🔊
Chúfēi yǒu wèntí, bùrán wǒ bú tuìhuò.
Unless there's a problem, I won't return it.
除非下雨，不然我走路去。🔊
Chúfēi xià yǔ, bùrán wǒ zǒulù qù.
Unless it rains, I'll walk there.
除非打折，不然太贵了。🔊
Chúfēi dǎzhé, bùrán tài guì le.
Unless it's discounted, it's too expensive.
