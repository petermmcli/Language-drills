Grammar

3. 开 (kāi) / 坐 (zuò) / 骑 (qí) / 走路 (zǒulù) — picking the verb by your role

English says "take the car" or "take the train" with one verb; Chinese asks who is steering. 开 (kāi) is for a vehicle you drive yourself, 坐 (zuò) for one you sit in as a passenger (bus, subway, train, taxi, high-speed rail), 骑 (qí) for a bicycle you sit on and pedal, and 走路 (zǒulù) needs no vehicle at all. Never stack two of them: 坐开车 (zuò kāi chē) is wrong — pick one. The destination follows with 去 (qù).

开车或坐公共汽车去别的地方。🔊
Kāi chē huò zuò gōnggòng qìchē qù bié de dìfang.
Drive or take the bus to other places.
我骑自行车去上班。🔊
Wǒ qí zìxíngchē qù shàngbān.
I ride a bicycle to work.
我开车去诊所。🔊
Wǒ kāi chē qù zhěnsuǒ.
I drive to the clinic.
少走路，多休息。🔊
Shǎo zǒulù, duō xiūxi.
Walk less, rest more.

5. 不太 (bú tài) vs 太…了 (tài… le) — "not very" vs "too"

不太 (bú tài) is a softened negative — it states a degree, so no 了 (le) is needed, and 不太远了 (bú tài yuǎn le) is wrong. 太…了 (tài… le) is a fixed frame: 太 (tài) goes first, the adjective sits in the middle, and 了 (le) closes it; that 了 (le) belongs to the frame, not to a completed action, so leaving it off makes the sentence sound cut off. 太…了 (tài… le) is not always a complaint — 太好了 (tài hǎo le) and 太快了 (tài kuài le) are admiring. 不太近 (bú tài jìn) and 不太远 (bú tài yuǎn) are opposites, so pick by what you mean; 不太 (bú tài) is also the polite way to disagree — gentler than a plain 很不 (hěn bú) + adjective.

不太远。🔊
Bú tài yuǎn.
Not very far.

6. 有点儿 (yǒudiǎnr) + adjective vs 早点儿 (zǎo diǎnr) + verb

有点儿 (yǒudiǎnr) describes how something is — 有点儿累 (yǒudiǎnr lèi), 有点儿贵 (yǒudiǎnr guì), 有点儿早 (yǒudiǎnr zǎo). To say you must do something earlier, you need an adjective plus 点儿 (diǎn r) in front of the verb: 早点儿走 (zǎo diǎnr zǒu). So 我有点儿早得去 (wǒ yǒudiǎnr zǎo děi qù) is not natural; say 我得早点儿走 (wǒ děi zǎo diǎnr zǒu) or 我得早一点儿走 (wǒ děi zǎo yí diǎn r zǒu). Keep 走 (zǒu) for "leave" and 去 (qù) for "go".

我得早点儿走。🔊
Wǒ děi zǎo diǎnr zǒu.
I have to leave a little early.

8. 对…没有帮助 (duì… méiyǒu bāngzhù) / 对…很好 (duì… hěn hǎo) — "X is (not) good for Y"

对 (duì) here introduces the thing affected — 对膝盖 (duì xīgài) "for the knee", 对身体 (duì shēntǐ) "for the body" — and the evaluation follows. 帮助 (bāngzhù) is a noun here ("help"), so the negative is 没有 (méiyǒu), "does not have", never 不 (bù). Do not confuse this 对 (duì) with 对吗 (duì ma)？ "right?": same character, different job.

吃药对膝盖没有帮助。🔊
Chī yào duì xīgài méiyǒu bāngzhù.
Taking medicine doesn't help the knee.
游泳对膝盖很好。🔊
Yóuyǒng duì xīgài hěn hǎo.
Swimming is very good for the knee.
休息对膝盖有帮助。🔊
Xiūxi duì xīgài yǒu bāngzhù.
Rest helps the knee.

9. 一共 (yígòng) and 只有 (zhǐyǒu) — "in total" and "only"

一共 (yígòng) collects everything into one figure — twenty minutes there plus twenty back plus the swim makes 一共要两个小时 (yígòng yào liǎng ge xiǎoshí). It does not combine with 都 (dōu): say 一共要两个小时 (yígòng yào liǎng ge xiǎoshí), not 都一共两个小时 (dōu yígòng liǎng ge xiǎoshí). 只有 (zhǐyǒu) is literally "only there is": 只 (zhǐ) limits and 有 (yǒu) says there is. It is neutral, unlike 有点少 (yǒudiǎn shǎo), which complains.

这个诊所只有两个医生。🔊
Zhège zhěnsuǒ zhǐyǒu liǎng ge yīshēng.
This clinic has only two doctors.
从我家到那儿一共要两个小时。🔊
Cóng wǒ jiā dào nàr yígòng yào liǎng ge xiǎoshí.
From my house to there it takes two hours in total.

10. 多 (duō) / 少 (shǎo) + verb — do more / do less

多 (duō) and 少 (shǎo) go before the verb, like adverbs, where English puts "more" or "less" after it. They say how much of an action, not how many things — for counting things you want 很多 (hěn duō) / 很少 (hěn shǎo) before a noun. This is the standard shape for short advice, which makes it useful with patients.

少走路，多休息。🔊
Shǎo zǒulù, duō xiūxi.
Walk less, rest more.
少开车，多骑车。🔊
Shǎo kāi chē, duō qí chē.
Drive less, ride more.

11. 先…再…最后… (xiān… zài… zuìhòu…) — chaining future steps

再 (zài) can repeat as many times as you need, so one sentence can carry a whole sequence. Open with 先 (xiān) "first" and close with 最后 (zuìhòu) "finally" for a clean chain. This works for directions too, where 到 (dào) marks the end: 到了 (dào le) "you're there". Remember that 再 (zài) only covers future or unfinished steps.

先吃饭，再洗澡，再看电视，最后睡觉。🔊
Xiān chī fàn, zài xǐzǎo, zài kàn diànshì, zuìhòu shuìjiào.
First eat, then bathe, then watch TV, finally sleep.
先往前走，再往右转，再走五分钟，就到了。🔊
Xiān wǎng qián zǒu, zài wǎng yòu zhuǎn, zài zǒu wǔ fēnzhōng, jiù dào le.
First go straight, then turn right, then walk five minutes, and you're there.

12. 然后 (ránhòu) / 就 (jiù) — chaining steps that already happened

再 (zài) is reserved for the future, so a sequence that is already finished takes 然后 (ránhòu) "then, afterwards", or 就 (jiù) when the second action followed immediately. 了 (le) marks the completed actions. Compare the future version, which is fine with 再 (zài): 吃完饭，再看电视 (chī wán fàn, zài kàn diànshì).

我昨天先吃了饭，然后看了电视。🔊
Wǒ zuótiān xiān chī le fàn, ránhòu kàn le diànshì.
Yesterday I ate first, then watched TV.
他下了班就回家了。🔊
Tā xià le bān jiù huí jiā le.
As soon as he got off work, he went home.

13. Verb + 了 + duration + 了 — started in the past, still going now

The first 了 (le), right after the verb, says the action happened; the final 了 (le), at the end of the sentence, says the situation is still true now. A single 了 (le) instead means it is over: 我学了两年中文 (wǒ xué le liǎng nián zhōng wén) = I studied Chinese for two years (and stopped). The usual cues are a length of time (两年 (liǎng nián), 一个小时 (yí ge xiǎoshí)) or 已经 (yǐjīng) "already", and the sentence normally has no other time word at the end.

我学了两年中文了。🔊
Wǒ xué le liǎng nián Zhōngwén le.
I've been studying Chinese for two years (and still am).
她已经等了半个小时了。🔊
Tā yǐjīng děng le bàn ge xiǎoshí le.
She's been waiting half an hour already.

16. 在 (zài) + place — before the verb, not after

Chinese sets the scene before the action, so 在 (zài) plus the place comes before the verb, where English puts it after: 不喜欢在伦敦开车 (bù xǐhuan zài Lúndūn kāi chē), not 不喜欢开车在伦敦 (bù xǐhuan kāi chē zài Lúndūn). The same order holds when a time word is present: subject, time, place, verb — 我星期二在医院做手术 (wǒ xīngqī'èr zài yīyuàn zuò shǒushù).

哥哥不喜欢在伦敦开车。🔊
Gēge bù xǐhuan zài Lúndūn kāi chē.
My brother doesn't like driving in London.
在加州停车很容易。🔊
Zài Jiāzhōu tíngchē hěn róngyì.
Parking is easy in California.

17. 不 (bù) vs 没有 (méiyǒu) — states and feelings vs "does not have"

Words that describe a state or a feeling — 怕 (pà), 重要 (zhòngyào), 贵 (guì) — take 不 (bù). 有 (yǒu) is a verb of possession and existence and only 没 (méi) negates it — never 不 (bù) — so 没有冬令时 (méiyǒu dōnglìngshí), 没有手术 (méiyǒu shǒushù), 没有朋友 (méiyǒu péng yǒu); 不有 (bú yǒu) sounds plainly wrong. 没问题 (méi wèntí) and 没关系 (méi guān xì) drop 有 (yǒu) and keep 没 (méi). The exception that looks like a counterexample is 对…没有帮助 (duì… méiyǒu bāngzhù): there 帮助 (bāngzhù) is a noun, so 没有 (méiyǒu) is correct even though the sentence is about a judgement, not a possession.

孩子们不怕。🔊
Háizimen bú pà.
The children aren't afraid.
快不重要。🔊
Kuài bú zhòngyào.
Speed isn't important.

18. Verb/adjective + 的 (de) — standing in for a dropped noun

收到 (shōudào) is "to receive", so 收到的 (shōudào de) is "the one that was received". The final 的 (de) after a color or a description carries the missing noun: 白色 (bái sè) is just the color word, 白色的 (bái sè de) is "a white one". Everyday nouns work the same way — 吃的 (chī de) is "things to eat" because 的 (de) holds the missing 东西 (dōng xī). Keep only one 的 (de) and make the pronoun match the speaker: 我要的地址 (wǒ yào de dìzhǐ), not 你的要的地址 (nǐ de yào de dìzhǐ).

她买了一件灰色的衣服，但是收到的是白色的。🔊
Tā mǎi le yí jiàn huīsè de yīfu, dànshì shōudào de shì báisè de.
She bought a grey top, but what she received was a white one.
买了一些吃的，用的，穿的。🔊
Mǎi le yìxiē chī de, yòng de, chuān de.
I bought some things to eat, use and wear.
这件太小，我要大号的。🔊
Zhè jiàn tài xiǎo, wǒ yào dàhào de.
This one is too small, I want a large.
给她换一件中号的。🔊
Gěi tā huàn yí jiàn zhōnghào de.
Exchange it for a medium one for her.

20. 要看… (yào kàn…) — "it depends on…"

看 (kàn) here is not "to watch" but "to look at what decides it", and 要 (yào) is "has to". 要看情况 (yào kàn qíngkuàng) is the ready-made answer when you do not want to name a factor. To name one, put a full clause after 看 (kàn), often ending in a question word: 要看你觉得怎么样 (yào kàn nǐ juéde zěnme yàng). Switch the pronoun to match whoever decides: 要看我觉得怎么样 (yào kàn wǒ juéde zěnme yàng).

要看情况。🔊
Yào kàn qíngkuàng.
It depends.
要看病人的情况。🔊
Yào kàn bìngrén de qíngkuàng.
It depends on the patient's situation.
你喜欢诊所还是手术？要看情况。🔊
Nǐ xǐhuan zhěnsuǒ háishi shǒushù? Yào kàn qíngkuàng.
Do you prefer the clinic or surgery? It depends.
要看你觉得怎么样。🔊
Yào kàn nǐ juéde zěnme yàng.
It depends on what you think.

26. ……就行 (jiù xíng) — "just…and that's it"

行 (xíng) means "will do", and 就 (jiù) says "that alone is enough". It ends a how-to neatly: do this one thing and you are finished. It is lighter than 可以 (kěyǐ) and friendlier than 必须 (bì xū). 就行了 (jiù xíng le) with 了 (le) is equally common and sounds a touch warmer.

以后要打车用这个APP就行。🔊
Yǐhòu yào dǎchē yòng zhège APP jiù xíng.
From now on, just use this app when you need a ride.
你写上地址就行了。🔊
Nǐ xiěshàng dìzhǐ jiù xíng le.
Just fill in the address and that's it.
点这儿就行了。🔊
Diǎn zhèr jiù xíng le.
Just tap here and that's it.
你下载这个App就行。🔊
Nǐ xiàzài zhège App jiù xíng.
Just download this app and that's it.
