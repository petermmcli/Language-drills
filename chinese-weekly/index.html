Grammar

1. Adjective + 多了 (duōle) ("much more") vs 得多 (de duō)

Adds "a lot more / much more" to a comparison, usually one already set up with 比 (bǐ). 多了 (duō le) is the casual form and needs the 了 (le), which marks the change or result — "it has become much more X." 得多 (de duō) says the same thing without 了 (le) and sounds slightly more neutral or written. The bare adjective works fine in a plain 比 (bǐ) comparison (汉语比广东话有用 (Hànyǔ bǐ Guǎngdōnghuà yǒuyòng)); 多 (duō) only appears when you want to say "much more," and once 多 (duō) is there the sentence feels incomplete without 了 (le) or the 得 (de) construction. Structure: Adjective + 多了 (duōle); or Adjective + 得多 (de duō).

汉语比广东话有用多了。🔊
Hànyǔ bǐ Guǎngdōnghuà yǒuyòng duō le.
Mandarin is much more useful than Cantonese.
今天比昨天冷多了。🔊
Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng duō le.
Today is much colder than yesterday.
这个城市比那个大多了。🔊
Zhège chéngshì bǐ nàge dà duō le.
This city is much bigger than that one.

2. 之一 (zhī yī) ("one of")

Marks something as one member of a group, equivalent to English "one of the ...". Unlike English, 之一 (zhī yī) comes at the END of the phrase, not before the noun. 之 (zhī) is a formal "of," like 的 (de), and 一 (yī) is "one"; it keeps its first tone because it ends the phrase. It is usually paired with a superlative such as 最 (zuì) or with 重要 (zhòngyào), and it is more written than spoken — in casual speech Chinese prefers 其中一个 (qízhōng yí ge) or simply rephrasing. Dropping 之一 (zhī yī) changes the meaning to "the one and only" (这是我最喜欢的书 (zhè shì wǒ zuì xǐhuan de shū) = my single favorite book). Structure: (最 + Adjective + 的) + Noun + 之一 (zhī yī).

北京是中国最大的城市之一。🔊
Běijīng shì Zhōngguó zuì dà de chéngshì zhī yī.
Beijing is one of China's biggest cities.
健康是最重要的事情之一。🔊
Jiànkāng shì zuì zhòngyào de shìqing zhī yī.
Health is one of the most important things.
这是我最喜欢的城市之一。🔊
Zhè shì wǒ zuì xǐhuan de chéngshì zhī yī.
This is one of my favorite cities.

3. Fractions and percentages with 分之 (fēn zhī)

Expresses fractions and percentages — the bottom number is said first. Literally "X parts, of which Y": 四分之一 (sì fēn zhī yī) (sì fēn zhī yī) is "one of four parts" = 1/4. 分 (fēn) is "part" and 之 (zhī) is the same formal "of" as in 之一 (zhī yī) — Chinese names the number of parts first, then the part you mean, the reverse of English "one quarter". Percentages use 百 (bǎi) (hundred) as the denominator, so 百分之五十 (bǎi fēn zhī wǔshí) is 50%. For one half, both 二分之一 (èr fēn zhī yī) and the everyday 一半 (yíbàn) work, with 一半 (yíbàn) far more common in speech. When the fraction describes a group of people or things, add 的 (de) plus the noun and the whole phrase becomes the subject — this carries the sense "some, not all": 四分之一的学生有手机 (sì fēn zhī yī de xuésheng yǒu shǒujī). Structure: Denominator + 分之 (fēn zhī) + Numerator (percent: 百分之 (bǎi fēn zhī) + Number); with a noun: Fraction + 的 (de) + Noun + Verb.

三分之二的学生是女生。🔊
Sān fēn zhī èr de xuésheng shì nǚshēng.
Two thirds of the students are girls.
百分之五十的人没有车。🔊
Bǎi fēn zhī wǔshí de rén méiyǒu chē.
Fifty percent of people don't have a car.
四分之一的学生有手机。🔊
Sì fēn zhī yī de xuésheng yǒu shǒujī.
A quarter of the students have a phone.
四分之一的病人是孩子。🔊
Sì fēn zhī yī de bìngrén shì háizi.
A quarter of the patients are children.

4. 刻 (kè): 一刻钟 (yí kè zhōng) (length of time) vs 七点一刻 (qī diǎn yí kè) (point on the clock)

Expresses quarter-hours, with two different forms depending on whether you mean how long something takes or what time it is. 刻 (kè) is "a quarter of an hour" and only exists as 一刻 (yí kè) and 三刻 (sān kè) — there is no 两刻 (liǎng kè), because two quarters is 半 (bàn). A length of time needs 钟 (zhōng) on the end (一刻钟 (yí kè zhōng) = 15 minutes, 三刻钟 (sān kè zhōng) = 45 minutes) and takes no 个 (ge) between 一 (yī) and 刻钟 (kè zhōng). A point on the clock drops 钟 (zhōng) and follows 点 (diǎn): 七点一刻 (qī diǎn yí kè) = 7:15. You can always fall back on the plain number (十五分钟 (shíwǔ fēnzhōng)) instead. 刻 (kè) is for time only — for a fraction of a group use 四分之一 (sì fēn zhī yī). Structure: Number + 刻钟 (kè zhōng) (a length: 一刻钟 (yí kè zhōng) / 三刻钟 (sān kè zhōng)); Hour + 点 (diǎn) + 一刻 (yí kè) (a clock time: 七点一刻 (qī diǎn yí kè)).

走路大概一刻钟。🔊
Zǒulù dàgài yí kè zhōng.
It is about a quarter of an hour on foot.
第一个手术七点一刻开始。🔊
Dì yī ge shǒushù qī diǎn yí kè kāishǐ.
The first surgery starts at 7:15.
我家到诊所开车大概三刻钟。🔊
Wǒ jiā dào zhěnsuǒ kāichē dàgài sān kè zhōng.
It is about 45 minutes by car from my home to the clinic.

5. 出 (chū) + PLACE: leaving a place

Says that you go out of or leave a place, as opposed to going to one. 出 (chū) means "out," so 出 (chū) + place names what you are stepping out of; 去 (qù) points at a destination instead. Stations and buildings you come out of take 出 (chū): 出地铁站 (chū dìtiězhàn), 出火车站 (chū huǒchēzhàn), 出公司 (chū gōngsī). The next action usually follows with 以后 (yǐhòu). Note that 出差 (chūchāi) is a separate whole word (a verb meaning "go on a business trip"), not 出 (chū) + something. Structure: 出 (chū) + place (+ 以后 (yǐhòu) + next action, or + 了 (le)).

我已经出火车站了。🔊
Wǒ yǐjīng chū huǒchēzhàn le.
I have already left the train station.
出诊所以后，我开车回家。🔊
Chū zhěnsuǒ yǐhòu, wǒ kāichē huí jiā.
After I leave the clinic, I drive home.
出地铁站以后，我走路回家。🔊
Chū dìtiězhàn yǐhòu, wǒ zǒulù huí jiā.
After I leave the subway station, I walk home.

6. 从……到…… (cóng… dào…): from … to …

Marks a span from one point to another, for places and for times alike. 从 (cóng) marks where you start and 到 (dào) where you arrive. For a journey, the means of travel goes between them and the duration goes last with 大概 (dàgài). The same frame works for clock times (从八点半到五点半 (cóng bā diǎn bàn dào wǔ diǎn bàn)) and for days (从星期一到星期五 (cóng xīngqīyī dào xīngqīwǔ)). Keep 到 (dào) for "arrive" and do not confuse it with 带 (dài), "take along". Structure: 从 (cóng) + start + (transport verb) + 到 (dào) + end + 大概 (dàgài) + duration.

从我家骑自行车到地铁站大概五分钟。🔊
Cóng wǒ jiā qí zìxíngchē dào dìtiězhàn dàgài wǔ fēnzhōng.
It is about five minutes from my home to the subway station by bike.
我的工作时间从八点半到五点半。🔊
Wǒ de gōngzuò shíjiān cóng bā diǎn bàn dào wǔ diǎn bàn.
My working hours are from 8:30 to 5:30.
从我家到机场开车大概四十分钟。🔊
Cóng wǒ jiā dào jīchǎng kāichē dàgài sìshí fēnzhōng.
It is about forty minutes by car from my home to the airport.

7. 在 (zài) + PLACE + VERB: where the action happens

States the location at which an action takes place. English puts the place last ("have a meeting at a restaurant"), but Chinese puts it before the verb: 在餐厅 (zài cāntīng) first, then 开会 (kāihuì). This is one of the most persistent English-order slips — 你工作在上海 (nǐ gōngzuò zài Shànghǎi) is wrong, 你在上海工作 (nǐ zài Shànghǎi gōngzuò) is right. Contrast 去 (qù) + place (a destination you are heading to) with 在 (zài) + place (where you already are). Structure: Subject + 在 (zài) + place + Verb.

我和同事在餐厅开会。🔊
Wǒ hé tóngshì zài cāntīng kāihuì.
I have a meeting at a restaurant with my colleague.
我在市中心的诊所工作。🔊
Wǒ zài shìzhōngxīn de zhěnsuǒ gōngzuò.
I work at a clinic in the city center.
她在家吃饭。🔊
Tā zài jiā chīfàn.
She eats at home.

8. 离 (lí): A is near / far from B

States the distance between two fixed places, without anyone travelling. 离 (lí) measures a gap between two points, so it pairs with 近 (jìn) and 远 (yuǎn) rather than with a duration. The place you measure from comes first. If you want to say how long the trip takes instead, switch to 从……到…… (cóng…dào…) plus a time. Structure: A + 离 (lí) + B + 很近 (hěn jìn) / 不太远 (bú tài yuǎn) / 远吗 (yuǎn ma).

我家离市中心很近。🔊
Wǒ jiā lí shìzhōngxīn hěn jìn.
My home is very close to the city center.
你家离公司远吗？🔊
Nǐ jiā lí gōngsī yuǎn ma?
Is your home far from work?
上海离苏州很近。🔊
Shànghǎi lí Sūzhōu hěn jìn.
Shanghai is very close to Suzhou.

9. 坐 (zuò) vs 骑 (qí), and 带 (dài) vs 到 (dào)

Picks the right verb for a means of transport, and keeps "bring" apart from "arrive". 坐 (zuò) is for vehicles you sit inside — subway, high-speed train, car as a passenger. 骑 (qí) is for things you sit on and straddle, so a bike is always 骑自行车 (qí zìxíngchē), never 坐自行车 (zuò zìxíngchē). 开车 (kāichē) is to drive, 骑车 (qí chē) is to ride. Separately, 带 (dài) is "take along, bring" and 到 (dào) is "arrive"; they sound alike but do different jobs. Structure: 坐 (zuò) + 地铁 (dìtiě) / 高铁 (gāotiě) / 火车 (huǒchē); 骑 (qí) + 自行车 (zìxíngchē) / 电动车 (diàndòngchē); 带 (dài) + thing + 上 (shàng) + vehicle; 到 (dào) + place.

我儿子骑自行车去学校，我坐火车去机场。🔊
Wǒ érzi qí zìxíngchē qù xuéxiào, wǒ zuò huǒchē qù jīchǎng.
My son rides a bike to school and I take the train to the airport.
你可以带自行车上高铁。🔊
Nǐ kěyǐ dài zìxíngchē shàng gāotiě.
You can take a bike onto the high-speed train.
她骑车五分钟就到了。🔊
Tā qí chē wǔ fēnzhōng jiù dào le.
She got there in only five minutes by bike.

10. 就 (jiù) "in only" vs 最后 (zuìhòu) "last", and 先……再……最后…… (xiān…zài…zuìhòu…)

就 (jiù) stresses that something happens quickly or in a short time; 最后 (zuìhòu) marks the final step of a sequence. 就 (jiù) is not a sequence word and does not mean "finally" — it emphasizes that the action took only that much time. For a list of steps, mark them 先 (xiān), then 再 (zài), then 最后 (zuìhòu). 然后 (ránhòu) is the looser "then". Mixing the two up is easy because both can sit near the end of a sentence, but they answer different questions: 就 (jiù) answers "how fast", 最后 (zuìhòu) answers "which step". Structure: Subject + duration + 就 (jiù) + Verb + 了 (le); 先 (xiān) + step 1，再 (zài) + step 2，最后 (zuìhòu) + step 3.

她骑车五分钟就到了。🔊
Tā qí chē wǔ fēnzhōng jiù dào le.
She got there in only five minutes by bike.
她先骑车，再走路，最后坐地铁。🔊
Tā xiān qí chē, zài zǒulù, zuìhòu zuò dìtiě.
First she rides a bike, then walks, and last takes the subway.
我走路十分钟就到诊所了。🔊
Wǒ zǒulù shí fēnzhōng jiù dào zhěnsuǒ le.
It takes me only ten minutes to walk to the clinic.

11. 怎么 (zěnme) vs 怎么样 (zěnmeyàng)

怎么 (zěnme) asks by what means something is done; 怎么样 (zěnmeyàng) asks what something is like. 怎么 (zěnme) sits straight in front of the verb and asks for the method, so the answer is 骑自行车 (qí zìxíngchē) or 坐地铁 (zuò dìtiě). 怎么样 (zěnmeyàng) comes after a noun and asks for an opinion or a condition, so the answer is 很好 (hěn hǎo) or 很方便 (hěn fāngbiàn). Dropping 样 (yàng) is exactly what is needed in a "how do you get there" question. Structure: 怎么 (zěnme) + Verb; Noun + 怎么样 (zěnmeyàng).

你每天怎么去公司？🔊
Nǐ měitiān zěnme qù gōngsī?
How do you get to work every day?
天气怎么样？🔊
Tiānqì zěnmeyàng?
How is the weather?
这个城市怎么样？🔊
Zhège chéngshì zěnmeyàng?
What is this city like?

12. 一个多小时 (yí ge duō xiǎoshí): Number + measure word + 多 (duō) + noun

Says "a little more than" that amount. 多 (duō) goes immediately after the measure word and before the noun, so the extra part is tacked onto the whole unit: 一个多小时 (yí ge duō xiǎoshí). Putting 多 (duō) at the end (一个小时多 (yí ge xiǎoshí duō)) or at the front (多一个小时 (duō yí ge xiǎoshí)) are both wrong. Compare 一个小时 (yí ge xiǎoshí) (exactly an hour) and 半个小时 (bàn ge xiǎoshí) (half an hour). Structure: Number + measure word + 多 (duō) + noun.

大概一个多小时。🔊
Dàgài yí ge duō xiǎoshí.
About an hour and a bit.
车多的时候，从我家到机场要一个多小时。🔊
Chē duō de shíhou, cóng wǒ jiā dào jīchǎng yào yí ge duō xiǎoshí.
With heavy traffic it takes more than an hour from my home to the airport.
我等了一个多小时。🔊
Wǒ děng le yí ge duō xiǎoshí.
I waited for a bit over an hour.

13. 听不清 (tīng bu qīng) vs 听不见 (tīng bu jiàn) / 听不到 (tīng bú dào) (and the same with 看 (kàn))

Says you cannot achieve the result of hearing or seeing, with the ending telling you in what way. 听 (tīng) and 看 (kàn) only name the action; the ending reports the result. 清 (qīng) says the signal arrives but is not clear — about 80 percent through, enough to make you ask "what did you say?". 见 (jiàn) and 到 (dào) say nothing arrives at all, 100 percent nothing; the two are interchangeable. Put 不 (bù) in the middle for "cannot get that result" (quite different from 不听 (bù tīng), "will not listen") and 得 (de) for "can". A patient behind a mask hears sound but cannot make it out, so 听不清 (tīng bu qīng) is the word. Keep these apart from 听不懂 (tīng bu dǒng), which is about failing to understand. Structure: Verb + 不 (bù) + result ending (听不清 (tīng bu qīng) / 听不见 (tīng bu jiàn) / 听不到 (tīng bú dào); 看不清 (kàn bu qīng) / 看不见 (kàn bu jiàn) / 看不到 (kàn bu dào)); positive: Verb + 得 (de) + ending (看得见 (kàn de jiàn)).

你戴口罩，可能你的病人听不清。🔊
Nǐ dài kǒuzhào, kěnéng nǐ de bìngrén tīng bu qīng.
With a mask on, your patients may not hear you clearly.
我戴眼镜，因为没有眼镜我看不清。🔊
Wǒ dài yǎnjìng, yīnwèi méiyǒu yǎnjìng wǒ kàn bu qīng.
I wear glasses because without them I cannot see clearly.
太远了，我听不见。🔊
Tài yuǎn le, wǒ tīng bu jiàn.
It is too far; I cannot hear anything.

14. 戴 (dài) vs 穿 (chuān): choosing the verb for "wear"

Picks the right "wear" verb according to how the item is worn. English uses one verb for everything, but Chinese chooses by the item. 穿 (chuān) is for clothes and shoes that you step or slide into; 戴 (dài) is for accessories you put on or attach — a mask, glasses, a hat, a watch, jewellery. 穿口罩 (chuān kǒuzhào) is wrong; it is always 戴口罩 (dài kǒuzhào). Structure: 戴 (dài) + mask / glasses / hat / watch; 穿 (chuān) + clothes / shoes.

不是穿口罩，戴口罩。🔊
Bú shì chuān kǒuzhào, dài kǒuzhào.
Not 穿 a mask, but 戴 a mask.
手术的时候我戴口罩。🔊
Shǒushù de shíhou wǒ dài kǒuzhào.
I wear a mask during surgery.
我每天戴眼镜。🔊
Wǒ měitiān dài yǎnjìng.
I wear glasses every day.

15. 可是 (kěshì) vs 但是 (dànshì): two words for "but"

Both join a contrast; the choice is a matter of tone. 但是 (dànshì) is neutral and the safe default. 可是 (kěshì) carries a small note of regret — a "what a pity" feeling — so it fits when the second clause spoils the first. Any sentence that takes 可是 (kěshì) can take 但是 (dànshì), but not always the other way round, so when in doubt use 但是 (dànshì). Structure: Sentence 1。可是 (kěshì) / 但是 (dànshì) + Sentence 2.

我想去外面吃饭。可是外面下雨了。🔊
Wǒ xiǎng qù wàimiàn chīfàn. Kěshì wàimiàn xià yǔ le.
I want to eat out, but it is raining.
我想休息，可是明天有手术。🔊
Wǒ xiǎng xiūxi, kěshì míngtiān yǒu shǒushù.
I want to rest, but I have surgery tomorrow.
但是我觉得可能三天。🔊
Dànshì wǒ juéde kěnéng sān tiān.
But I think it may be three days.

16. 出差 (chūchāi) is a verb phrase, not a noun

Says that someone goes away on a business trip. 有 (yǒu) is for having a noun (有车 (yǒu chē), 有时间 (yǒu shíjiān)), and 出差 (chūchāi) is not a noun, so 有出差 (yǒu chūchāi) does not work. Say that someone 去出差 (qù chūchāi) or 要出差 (yào chūchāi) instead. When you name the destination it goes in the middle: 去 (qù) + place + 出差 (chūchāi). Structure: Subject + 要 (yào) / 去 (qù) (+ place) + 出差 (chūchāi).

我早上要去深圳出差。🔊
Wǒ zǎoshang yào qù Shēnzhèn chūchāi.
I have to go to Shenzhen on a business trip in the morning.
下个星期我要去上海出差。🔊
Xià ge xīngqī wǒ yào qù Shànghǎi chūchāi.
Next week I will go to Shanghai on a business trip.
他下个星期去出差。🔊
Tā xià ge xīngqī qù chūchāi.
He is going away on business next week.

17. 想 (xiǎng) + person vs 想让 (xiǎng ràng) + person + verb

Says you want someone else to do something. 想 (xiǎng) followed by a person on its own means "miss" — 想你 (xiǎng nǐ) is "I miss you". To say you want another person to act, insert 让 (ràng), which covers both "let" and "ask". The person named after 想让 (xiǎng ràng) is the one who performs the verb. 要 (yào) can replace 想 (xiǎng) here with much the same effect, and the whole pattern reads as a polite request. Structure: 想让 (xiǎng ràng) + person + Verb.

我想让我的朋友去找你看看。🔊
Wǒ xiǎng ràng wǒ de péngyou qù zhǎo nǐ kànkan.
I would like my friend to come to you for a look.
明天我让司机送我们去机场吧。🔊
Míngtiān wǒ ràng sījī sòng wǒmen qù jīchǎng ba.
Tomorrow I will have the driver take us to the airport.
我想让你帮我。🔊
Wǒ xiǎng ràng nǐ bāng wǒ.
I want you to help me.

18. 一个是……另一个是…… (yí ge shì…lìng yí ge shì…) and 对方 (duìfāng)

Lists two things or two people as a pair. 一个 (yí ge) opens the pair and 另一个 (lìng yí ge) closes it — 另 (lìng) means "other" and ties the second half to the first. Numbering them with 第一个 (dì yī ge) and 第二个 (dì èr ge) works just as well, and is handy for retelling a story. Do not reach for 对方 (duìfāng) here: 对方 (duìfāng) is the other person in a conversation or exchange (when we two talk, you are my 对方 (duìfāng)), not "the other one of two". Structure: 一个是 (yí ge shì) A，另一个是 (lìng yí ge shì) B; or 第一个是 (dì yī ge shì) A，第二个是 (dì èr ge shì) B.

一个是九点二十分，另一个是八点五十分。🔊
Yí ge shì jiǔ diǎn èrshí fēn, lìng yí ge shì bā diǎn wǔshí fēn.
One is 9:20 and the other is 8:50.
我今天有两个手术。第一个是七点，第二个是十点。🔊
Wǒ jīntiān yǒu liǎng ge shǒushù. Dì yī ge shì qī diǎn, dì èr ge shì shí diǎn.
I have two surgeries today. The first is at seven, the second at ten.
现在我们说话，那你是对方。🔊
Xiànzài wǒmen shuōhuà, nà nǐ shì duìfāng.
We are talking now, so you are the other party.

19. 得 (de) between a verb and an adjective, vs a clock time before the verb

得 (de) describes how an action is done; a clock time before the verb says when it is done. 得 (de) links a verb to an adjective that rates it — 起得很早 (qǐ de hěn zǎo), 睡得很晚 (shuì de hěn wǎn), 说得很快 (shuō de hěn kuài). A specific clock time is a different kind of information and sits before the verb instead: 在六点半起床 (zài liù diǎn bàn qǐchuáng). Do not try to combine them (起得六点半 (qǐ de liù diǎn bàn) is wrong). When the verb has an object, repeat the verb before 得 (de): 说北京话说得很快 (shuō Běijīnghuà shuō de hěn kuài). Structure: Verb + 得 (de) + 很 (hěn) + adjective; for a time: (在) + clock time + Verb.

他起得很早，睡得也不晚。🔊
Tā qǐ de hěn zǎo, shuì de yě bù wǎn.
He gets up very early and does not go to bed late either.
司机说北京话说得很快。🔊
Sījī shuō Běijīnghuà shuō de hěn kuài.
The driver spoke Beijing dialect very fast.
我每天六点半起床。🔊
Wǒ měitiān liù diǎn bàn qǐchuáng.
I get up at 6:30 every day.

20. 对 (duì) + noun + 好 (hǎo) / 不好 (bù hǎo): good or bad for something

Says that something benefits or harms the thing named. 对 (duì) introduces whatever is affected and 好 (hǎo) or 不好 (bù hǎo) delivers the verdict. The commonest use is 对身体好 (duì shēntǐ hǎo), "good for your health". The fixed saying 早睡早起身体好 (zǎo shuì zǎo qǐ shēntǐ hǎo) drops 对 (duì) because it is a set phrase and is never altered; swap 早 (zǎo) and 晚 (wǎn) in each half for the variants 晚睡晚起 (wǎn shuì wǎn qǐ) and 晚睡早起 (wǎn shuì zǎo qǐ). Structure: 对 (duì) + noun + 好 (hǎo) / 不好 (bù hǎo).

多休息对身体好。🔊
Duō xiūxi duì shēntǐ hǎo.
Resting more is good for your health.
晚睡对我的身体不好。🔊
Wǎn shuì duì wǒ de shēntǐ bù hǎo.
Going to bed late is bad for my health.
我们中国人说早睡早起身体好。🔊
Wǒmen Zhōngguórén shuō zǎo shuì zǎo qǐ shēntǐ hǎo.
We Chinese say that early to bed and early to rise is good for health.
