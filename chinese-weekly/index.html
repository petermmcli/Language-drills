Grammar

1. 往 (wǎng) — Moving toward ("in the direction of")

往 (wǎng) sits in front of the verb and names the direction the movement goes. What follows 往 (wǎng) must be a direction or a place word (前 (qián), 左 (zuǒ), 右 (yòu), 这儿 (zhèr)), never a bare person — 往我 (wǎng wǒ) is wrong, 往我这儿 (wǎng wǒ zhèr) is right, because 这儿 (zhèr) turns "me" into "the place where I am". The verb depends on what moves: 开 (kāi) for a vehicle, 走 (zǒu) for a person on foot, 转 (zhuǎn) for turning. Adding 一点儿 (yìdiǎnr) softens it into a polite request, usually framed as 您可以…吗 (nín kěyǐ… ma) or …行吗 (…xíng ma). Structure: 往 (wǎng) + direction/place + Verb (+ 一点儿 (yìdiǎnr)).

师傅，您现在离我很近。您可以往前开一点儿吗？🔊
Shīfu, nín xiànzài lí wǒ hěn jìn. Nín kěyǐ wǎng qián kāi yìdiǎnr ma?
Driver, you're very close to me now. Can you drive forward a little?
不好意思，前面不可以停车。您可以往我这儿走一点儿，行吗？🔊
Bù hǎoyìsi, qiánmiàn bù kěyǐ tíngchē. Nín kěyǐ wǎng wǒ zhèr zǒu yìdiǎnr, xíng ma?
Sorry, there's no parking up ahead. Could you walk over toward me a little, okay?
请往前走。🔊
Qǐng wǎng qián zǒu.
Please walk forward.

2. 儿 (ér) in Beijing speech: 地儿 (dìr) = 地方 (dìfang)

The 儿 (ér) ending curls the final syllable and marks the speech as Beijing and informal; the meaning is unchanged. In the role-play the driver asks fast, in Beijing speech, 您在上车的地儿吗 (nín zài shàngchē de dìr ma)？ then repeats it slowly in standard Mandarin as 您在上车的地方吗 (nín zài shàngchē de dìfang ma)？ 地儿 (dìr) and 地方 (dìfang) mean exactly the same thing. The 儿 (ér) is not a separate word — it colors the preceding syllable (dì (dì) becomes dìr (dìr)). Some 儿 (ér) words are standard everywhere (这儿 (zhèr), 那儿 (nàr), 哪儿 (nǎr), 一点儿 (yìdiǎnr)); others like 地儿 (dìr) are mainly heard in the north, which is why a Beijing driver can be so much harder to follow than classroom 普通话 (pǔtōnghuà). Structure: Word + 儿 (ér) (地儿 (dìr), 这儿 (zhèr), 那儿 (nàr), 哪儿 (nǎr), 一点儿 (yìdiǎnr)).

您在上车的地儿吗？🔊
Nín zài shàngchē de dìr ma?
Are you at the pick-up spot?
您在哪儿呢？🔊
Nín zài nǎr ne?
Where are you?
这儿有很多车。🔊
Zhèr yǒu hěn duō chē.
There are a lot of cars here.

3. 或者 (huòzhě) vs 还是 (háishi) ("or": statements vs questions)

Both mean "or", but 或者 (huòzhě) lists acceptable options in a statement while 还是 (háishi) asks the listener to choose. If the speaker is saying "either one will do", use 或者 (huòzhě): 可以用微信或者支付宝付钱 (kěyǐ yòng Wēixìn huòzhě Zhīfùbǎo fùqián). If the speaker wants an answer to "which one?", use 还是 (háishi): 红酒还是白酒 (hóngjiǔ háishi báijiǔ)？ A quick test: question mark means 还是 (háishi); a full stop means 或者 (huòzhě). This is a recurring slip — 微信还是支付宝付钱 (Wēixìn háishi Zhīfùbǎo fùqián) as a statement had to be corrected to 微信或者支付宝付钱 (Wēixìn huòzhě Zhīfùbǎo fùqián), while the 还是 (háishi) questions in the same lesson were all correct. Structure: Statement: A 或者 (huòzhě) B. Question: A 还是 (háishi) B?.

可以用微信或者支付宝付钱。🔊
Kěyǐ yòng Wēixìn huòzhě Zhīfùbǎo fùqián.
You can pay with WeChat or Alipay.
你星期二还是星期四来诊所？🔊
Nǐ xīngqī'èr háishi xīngqīsì lái zhěnsuǒ?
Are you coming to the clinic on Tuesday or Thursday?
你可以坐出租车或者坐地铁。🔊
Nǐ kěyǐ zuò chūzūchē huòzhě zuò dìtiě.
You can take a taxi or take the subway.

4. 让 (ràng) — having someone do something

The person right after 让 (ràng) is the one who performs the following verb, so in 我让司机送我们去机场 (wǒ ràng sījī sòng wǒmen qù jīchǎng) it is the 司机 (sījī) who does the 送 (sòng). 让 (ràng) covers both permission ("let") and arrangement ("have someone do it"); context decides which. A sentence-final 吧 (ba) turns it into a suggestion. Keep it apart from 叫 (jiào) in 我会叫一个女司机 (wǒ huì jiào yí ge nǚ sījī), where 叫 (jiào) means "to call/hail a car", and from 帮 (bāng) + person + verb, which asks for help rather than arranging it. Structure: Subject + 让 (ràng) + person + Verb (+ object).

明天我让司机送我们去机场吧。🔊
Míngtiān wǒ ràng sījī sòng wǒmen qù jīchǎng ba.
Tomorrow let's have the driver take us to the airport.
我让护士给你打电话。🔊
Wǒ ràng hùshi gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.
I'll have the nurse call you.
妈妈让我洗碗。🔊
Māma ràng wǒ xǐ wǎn.
Mom has me wash the dishes.

5. A 离 (lí) B + (很) + 近 (jìn)/远 (yuǎn) (distance between two points)

离 (lí) compares two locations and is always followed by an adjective of distance — 很近 (hěn jìn), 不远 (bù yuǎn) — not by a verb of motion. It does not describe a journey, only the gap. Pair it with 从…到… (cóng… dào…) when you also want to say how long the trip takes. Note 两分钟以后到 (liǎng fēnzhōng yǐhòu dào): 两 (liǎng) (not 二 (èr)) is used before a measure word, and 以后 (yǐhòu) placed after a time phrase means "in two minutes' time". Structure: A + 离 (lí) + B + (很/不) + 近 (jìn) / 远 (yuǎn).

我离你不远，两分钟以后到。🔊
Wǒ lí nǐ bù yuǎn, liǎng fēnzhōng yǐhòu dào.
I'm not far from you; I'll be there in two minutes.
我家离诊所很近。🔊
Wǒ jiā lí zhěnsuǒ hěn jìn.
My home is very close to the clinic.
学校离我家不远。🔊
Xuéxiào lí wǒ jiā bù yuǎn.
The school isn't far from my house.

6. 从…到… (cóng… dào…) + 要 (yào)/需要 (xūyào) + time (how long a trip takes)

Where 离 (lí) measures a static gap, 从…到… (cóng… dào…) traces a route with a start and an end, which is why it pairs naturally with 要多长时间 (yào duōcháng shíjiān). 要 (yào) and 需要 (xūyào) both mean "takes (time)" here; 需要 (xūyào) is slightly more formal, and both appeared in the airport dialogue. The whole 从…到… (cóng… dào…) phrase comes before the verb, never after it. Structure: 从 (cóng) + start + 到 (dào) + end + 要 (yào) / 需要 (xūyào) + length of time.

从你家到T3要多长时间？🔊
Cóng nǐ jiā dào T sān yào duōcháng shíjiān?
How long does it take from your place to T3?
从我家到T3需要40分钟。🔊
Cóng wǒ jiā dào T sān xūyào sìshí fēnzhōng.
From my home to T3 takes 40 minutes.
从家到学校要十分钟。🔊
Cóng jiā dào xuéxiào yào shí fēnzhōng.
It takes ten minutes from home to school.

7. 找 (zhǎo) vs 教 (jiāo) (go and see someone vs teach)

找 (zhǎo) is "to look for" or "to go and find someone"; 教 (jiāo) is "to teach". In the favor call, 想去找你看看 (xiǎng qù zhǎo nǐ kànkan) means "he'd like to come to you to be looked at" — 看看 (kànkan) here is the informal way to talk about a medical check, which is why the caller's sick friend wants a doctor friend rather than a hospital. They differ in initial (zh- (zh-) vs j- (j-)) and in tone, so drill them as a pair. Related: 找不到 (zhǎo bu dào) means "can't find", and 怎么用 (zěnme yòng) — not 怎么做 (zěnme zuò) — is "how to use". Structure: 找 (zhǎo) + person (+ 看看 (kànkan)) — go to someone; 教 (jiāo) + person + skill — teach someone.

我有个朋友生病了，想去找你看看。🔊
Wǒ yǒu ge péngyou shēngbìng le, xiǎng qù zhǎo nǐ kànkan.
A friend of mine is ill; he'd like to come to you for a look.
老师教我怎么用滴滴。🔊
Lǎoshī jiāo wǒ zěnme yòng Dīdī.
The teacher taught me how to use Didi.
他想找老师问问题。🔊
Tā xiǎng zhǎo lǎoshī wèn wèntí.
He wants to go find the teacher to ask a question.

8. Verb + 懂 (dǒng): 听懂 (tīng dǒng) / 听不懂 (tīng bu dǒng) (understanding as a result)

听 (tīng) names the action and 懂 (dǒng) reports the outcome, so 听懂 (tīng dǒng) is "heard and understood" and 听不懂 (tīng bu dǒng) is "I can hear it but it makes no sense". The same ending works with other verbs of perception: 看不懂 (kàn bu dǒng) for something written. Keep it apart from 听不到 (tīng bu dào), which means the sound never reached you at all. The passenger uses 我听不懂 (wǒ tīng bu dǒng) on the driver's fast Beijing speech — the natural thing to say when someone speaks too fast. Structure: Verb + 懂 (dǒng) (achieved); Verb + 不 (bù) + 懂 (dǒng) (cannot).

您说什么？我听不懂。🔊
Nín shuō shénme? Wǒ tīng bu dǒng.
What did you say? I can't understand.
这个字我看不懂。🔊
Zhège zì wǒ kàn bu dǒng.
I can't make out this character.
我看懂了这本书。🔊
Wǒ kàn dǒng le zhè běn shū.
I understood this book.

9. 所有的…都… (suǒyǒu de… dōu…) (all … every one)

所有的 (suǒyǒu de) and 都 (dōu) work as a pair — 所有的 (suǒyǒu de) marks the group and 都 (dōu) must still appear right before the verb. Dropping 都 (dōu) is a common slip: 所有的师傅有五星好评 (suǒyǒu de shīfu yǒu wǔ xīng hǎopíng) needs to be 所有的师傅都有五星好评 (suǒyǒu de shīfu dōu yǒu wǔ xīng hǎopíng). 都 (dōu) always sits after the subject and before the verb, never at the end. Structure: 所有的 (suǒyǒu de) + Noun + 都 (dōu) + Verb/Adjective.

所有的师傅都有五星好评。🔊
Suǒyǒu de shīfu dōu yǒu wǔ xīng hǎopíng.
All the drivers have five-star ratings.
所有的病人都有家人。🔊
Suǒyǒu de bìngrén dōu yǒu jiārén.
All the patients have family.
所有的学生都很努力。🔊
Suǒyǒu de xuésheng dōu hěn nǔlì.
All the students are very hardworking.

10. 每 (měi) + measure word + noun (every …)

每 (měi) needs a measure word before the noun — 每个人 (měi ge rén), 每天 (měi tiān), 每次 (měi cì) — unlike 所有的 (suǒyǒu de), which attaches straight to the noun. When 每 (měi) is the subject, 都 (dōu) usually follows before the verb, the same pairing as 所有的…都… (suǒyǒu de… dōu…). 每 (měi) emphasizes the items individually; 所有的 (suǒyǒu de) takes the group as a whole. Structure: 每 (měi) + 量词 (liàngcí) + Noun (+ 都 (dōu) + Verb).

每个师傅都有五星好评。🔊
Měi ge shīfu dōu yǒu wǔ xīng hǎopíng.
Every driver has a five-star rating.
我每天都坐出租车去医院。🔊
Wǒ měi tiān dōu zuò chūzūchē qù yīyuàn.
I take a taxi to the hospital every day.
每个人都有手机。🔊
Měi ge rén dōu yǒu shǒujī.
Everyone has a mobile phone.
