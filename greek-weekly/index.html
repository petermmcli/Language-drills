Grammar — priority

What the teacher spent time on.

A The imperative: πίνε vs να πίνεις (most time spent here)taught

Pattern: the imperative verb form (πίνε, σηκώσου...) or να + present-tense verb (να πίνεις, να σηκώνεσαι). Negative: να μην + verb, or μην + verb.

Meaning: the learner asked whether «πίνε» and «να πίνεις» mean the same. Teacher: yes, the same thing, but «πίνε» is a little stronger, more of a direct command. «Να πίνεις» is softer, more like advice.

How and why it works: «να πίνεις» is just να + the ordinary 'you' form of the present, so you do not have to learn a separate imperative shape. That is why the teacher's practical rule was: when in doubt, use «να»; you will never go wrong. In the passive/middle (σηκώνομαι, κοιμάμαι) the teacher said only the «να» version is used, so να σηκώνεσαι, να κοιμάσαι.

Negative: the «να» is optional. Both «να μην πίνεις» and «μην πίνεις» are fine; the teacher said 'you decide'. The learner's exception question: you cannot say «οδηγά» (an invented imperative form); the teacher said it is better to say «να οδηγείς».

Examples from the lesson (teacher):

Να μην οδηγείς.
Don't drive.
Να μην περπατάς.
Don't walk.
Να μην πίνεις.
Don't drink.
Να μην σηκώνεσαι.
Don't get up.
Να μην κοιμάσαι.
Don't sleep.
Μπορείς να πεις και «μην πίνεις», «μην σηκώνεσαι», «μην κοιμάσαι» χωρίς το «να».
You can also say 'don't drink', 'don't get up', 'don't sleep' without the 'να'.

Additional simple examples (mine, not from the lesson):

Πίνε νερό!
Drink water! (direct, stronger)
Να πίνεις νερό.
You should drink water. (softer, advice)

Compare / contrast: Πίνε (strong, direct) vs Να πίνεις (softer, safe default). Να μην πίνεις vs Μην πίνεις: same meaning, να is just optional.

Common learner mistake: inventing an imperative that does not exist (οδηγά). Fix: fall back to να + present (να οδηγείς). One short line of caution: the learner's remark about 'plural' was unclear in the automatic transcript, but the teacher's answer was about the negative, so I follow the teacher's answer.

B γουστάρω: a verb with no aoristtaught

Pattern: γουστάρω (present), γούσταρα (past). That is all. There is no «γουστάρισα», no «γουστάρωσα» and no «έχω γουσταρέψει»; the teacher repeated this several times.

Meaning: very colloquial 'I really like / I'm into'. It can be said about a person (romantic or just a cool guy), a food, an adventure or an experience. The teacher's example about a person: a man says «τη γουστάρω αυτή» about a woman; a woman says «τον γουστάρω αυτόν».

How and why it works: the teacher's logic is that you cannot 'like' something one single time, so the one-time past (aorist) is not used. The past is the continuous one: «γούσταρα» = 'I liked it / I was into it'. Notice the learner called the 'aorist' an 'imperfect' in the question; the teacher answered only «γούσταρα» exists.

Examples from the lesson (teacher):

Τη γουστάρω αυτή.
I really like her.
Τον γουστάρω αυτόν.
I really like him.
Τον γούσταρα.
I was into him / I liked him.

Additional simple examples (mine):

Γουστάρω αυτό το φαγητό.
I really like this food.

Compare: γουστάρω (colloquial, strong) vs μου αρέσει (neutral). Both can be used for people, food, experiences.

Common learner mistake: forming a regular aorist (-ισα / -ωσα) from a regular -άρω verb. Fix: use «γούσταρα».

C Song idioms: τα βάζω στα πόδια, μας δένει στα παλιά, φτηνά τη γλίτωσαtaught

The teacher said that in Greek songs the meaning comes more from music, nostalgia and feeling than from literal sense.

«Τα βάζω στα πόδια» = I run away. The learner guessed «φεύγω» and the teacher added «φεύγω μακριά, τρέχω».

«Ό,τι μας δένει στα παλιά» = whatever ties us to the past. The teacher translated: 'it ties us to the past'.

«Φτηνά τη γλίτωσα» = I made a big mistake but did not pay for it, I had no consequences. «Φτηνά» is cheaply; «γλιτώνω» is to be safe from something. The learner then asked about «γλιτώνω κάποιον από κάτι» and the teacher confirmed it works.

Τι σημαίνει «δένει στα παλιά»;
What does 'ties to the past' mean?
Φτηνά τη γλίτωσα.
I got off cheaply / I got off lightly.

Why it matters: these are fixed expressions. Learn them whole. The «τη» in «τη γλίτωσα» is part of the idiom (my observation; the teacher did not break it down).

Common learner mistake: translating word by word. 'Cheap' alone would not explain the sentence.

D Word choice and collocations: παίρνω συνέντευξη, βολικό, φωτογράφοςcorrection

Three small corrections that share one rule: Greek picks its own verb or adjective, so do not translate from English.

«Παίρνω συνέντευξη» (not «κάνω»): the teacher said we usually do not say 'make'; we 'take' an interview.

«Βολικό» for 'convenient': the learner said «άνετο» and asked if it meant convenient; the teacher answered «δεν είναι πολύ βολικό».

«Φωτογράφος» is used for a woman too; the learner tried «φωτογράφη» and «φωτογράφ-» and the teacher confirmed «φωτογράφος».

Teacher also recast «επαγγελματικός» to «επαγγελματικό» (neuter, agreeing with the neuter idea 'this is not professional').

Δεν κάνω. Παίρνω συνέντευξη.
Not 'make'. You 'take' an interview.
Δεν είναι πολύ βολικό.
It is not very convenient.
Αυτό δεν είναι τόσο επαγγελματικό.
This is not so professional.

Common learner mistake: transferring English verbs (make an interview) and English-shaped feminine job titles.

E Agreement: προτιμά το κοινό / συμφωνούνfix

Teacher corrected «συμφωνεί» to «συμφωνούν» when the learner said 'half the country agrees'. In Greek the verb often follows the meaning (many people), so it goes plural, while «το κοινό προτιμά» stays singular because «κοινό» is a singular noun.

My note (not taught): συμφωνώ takes με + accusative.

Συμφωνούν με τον πρόεδρο.
They agree with the president.
Την τηλεόραση προτιμά το κοινό για τις ειδήσεις.
The public prefers television for the news.

Common learner mistake: hesitating between singular and plural for collective subjects. This is the teacher's correction and only this one example was given; the 'meaning plural' rule is my explanation.

Grammar that was not taught

  • Tenses and aspect: only one point taught (γουστάρω has no aorist). Present vs aorist stems were not taught.
  • Passive voice: only mentioned for the imperative (passive uses να). Not otherwise taught.
  • Subjunctive and να constructions: only να + present as a stand-in for the imperative. Other uses not taught.
  • Participles: not taught. The learner used «κοιτάζοντας» once without comment.
  • Cases: not taught. Only «συμφωνώ με + accusative» appeared in passing.
  • Pronouns: only the doubling in «τη γουστάρω αυτή / τον γουστάρω αυτόν», shown in context, not explained.
  • Conditionals: not taught. The learner used «αν δεν μου αρέσει...» correctly and was not corrected.
  • Connectors: not taught.

Where to focus next (teacher's diagnosis)

Evidence is moderate: about 20 minutes of the lesson were in Greek from the learner, with many switches to English. Only clear errors or teacher corrections are counted; transcription noise is ignored.

1. Gender, number and case agreement of articles and adjectives — About 4 times.

  • Said: Αυτό δεν είναι τόσο επαγγελματικός.
  • Correct: Αυτό δεν είναι τόσο επαγγελματικό. (This is not so professional. (teacher recast))
  • Said: ένα άντρας
  • Correct: ένας άντρας (a man)
  • Said: στο τσάντα μου
  • Correct: στην τσάντα μου (in my bag)

Rule: Check the noun's gender first, then make article and adjective match it; the object of a preposition takes the accusative («έναν άντρα», «στην τσάντα»).

2. Wrong verb or collocation copied from English — 3 times (one corrected by the teacher, two supplied by the teacher).

  • Said: κάνω συνέντευξη
  • Correct: παίρνω συνέντευξη (to interview)
  • Said: άνετο
  • Correct: βολικό (convenient)

Rule: Learn nouns together with their verbs (παίρνω συνέντευξη), not as translations.

3. Made-up word forms when the real one is not known — 3 times.

  • Said: οδηγά
  • Correct: να οδηγείς (drive!)
  • Said: γουστάρισα / γουστάρωσα
  • Correct: γούσταρα (I liked it)
  • Said: φωτογράφη
  • Correct: φωτογράφος (photographer (f.))

Rule: When unsure of an imperative or past form, use the safe frame (να + present, or a known past) instead of inventing a form.

4. Subject-verb agreement with collective subjects — Once, corrected by the teacher.

  • Said: μισή της χώρας συμφωνεί
  • Correct: συμφωνούν (they agree)

Rule: Check whether the verb should follow the meaning (many people, plural).

What you did well:

  • Conditional and 'to' structures: «Αν δεν μου αρέσει το Claude, είναι δύσκολο να αλλάξω.»
  • Να-clauses with plans: «Σήμερα έχω σχέδιο να πάω στο YouTube και να βλέπω τα βίντεο.»

What you avoided:

  • Subjunctive for a polite request: instead of English, say «Μπορείς να μου στείλεις το λινκ;» for 'Can you send me the link?'.
  • Explaining ideas in Greek: where the learner said 'I'll send it to you', she could say «Θα στο στείλω.»
  • Opinions: the learner switched to English for 'It's crazy'; she could say «Είναι τρελό.»

Practice this week:

  • Agreement drill: write 10 nouns with article and adjective (e.g. ένας καλός άντρας, μια μικρή τσάντα) and check each gender before saying them.
  • Collocation drill: make a list of 10 verb + noun pairs (παίρνω συνέντευξη, κάνω ερώτηση...) and say one sentence for each.
  • Imperative drill: give 6 orders using να + present and 6 using μην + verb, with no invented forms.

Short review plan

  1. Session 1 (10 min): imperative drill, 6 positive and 6 negative sentences, using να and μην.
  2. Session 2 (10 min): agreement drill from the top pattern; describe 5 photos with article + noun + adjective.
  3. Session 3 (10 min): song idioms and γουστάρω: write 3 sentences with each; say them aloud.
  4. Session 4 (10 min): collocations and news vocabulary: ενημερώνομαι, παίρνω συνέντευξη, βολικό, περιοδικό.
  5. Final speaking challenge (3 minutes): talk about how you keep informed every day and how Americans do it (this is also the teacher's exercise 3 homework).
